Engineering

What is designed, what has been checked on paper, and what is still waiting for the bench. Everything is at TRL 2: modeled, not built.

Where each piece stands
Designed4
Fines lung

A covered bay for 1 to 4 mm fines, a gas cooling train with a dew point set point, and two bays that take turns.

Pulsed bed reactor

Two fluidized slag beds in series, slag moving against the water, pulsed to keep the grains clean.

Ring, impeller and baffle units

A 50 degree ring, a slow impeller, and four hollow baffle units with a dip that catches the inert grains.

Plates and clarifier

60 degree settling plates in the reactor, aeration between the stages, a clarifier for the iron floc.

Checked on paper4
Grain sorting sizes

The bed lifts slag below about 90 µm and dense inert grains below about 67 µm at 9 mm/s. Worked out from settling speeds, to check against the bench.

Published carbon-uptake data

Figures from published lab work are re-derived from their stated conditions before they go into the model.

Cost model

A lean workbook for the three ideas. Every input is cited or flagged as a placeholder.

Review rounds

The design went through many rounds of review and was cut from a long list of ideas to three.

Waiting for the bench4
Test F5

Free lime and swelling of carbonated fines in a covered bed. Follows the work Dr. McCormick sets.

Leach test

Does the spent slag pass a swell test and a sulfate leach? It decides where it can go.

Pulsed bed on real mine water

A bench pulsed bed, to test the sorting and the slag use against the model.

Real prices

A vendor quote for equipment and a slag processor's figures, to replace the placeholders.

None of the planned tests has been run. They come after the work Dr. McCormick sets.

What moves the answer

Slag use decides whether it beats lime.

In the model, the share of the slag that actually reacts matters most. Moving the slag against the water is meant to raise it from about 40% to about 70%, and that is the difference between a thin margin and a better one.

If the spent slag has to go to landfill instead of back to the processor, the model loses to lime either way. That is what the leach test is for.

40% slag use~$120 / day
70% slag use~$280 / day
Margin over lime after capital, 10,000 m³/day example site. Desk model with placeholder prices, not measured.
