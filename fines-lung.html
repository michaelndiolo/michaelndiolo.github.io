Path 1 · at the steel mill

The fines lung

Fresh steel-slag fines swell when they get wet, so the fine sizes cannot go into roads or concrete. A covered bay and cooled stack gas can age them in days, not months. Modeled on paper: nothing is built and nothing is measured.

The idea

Free lime is what makes fresh slag swell. Stack gas turns it into calcium carbonate, which does not.

How a cycle runs

Screen the fines

Fresh fines go over a screen. What falls through, under 1 mm, goes to the silo and is trucked to the mine (path 2). The 1 to 4 mm fraction goes to the bay stockpile.

Cool and dry the gas

A fan pulls a share of the mill's stack gas into a knockout tower. Chilled water sprays down, the gas cools, and its extra water drops out. The gas leaves 1 to 3 °C below the bay's temperature, so the slag stays damp but nothing condenses on it.

Blow it up through the bed

Gas enters through floor trenches and rises through the fines. The bottom finishes first. Drip lines add water only when the probes ask.

Stop when the gas stops being used

When CO₂ at the outlet climbs to match the inlet, the free lime is spent. The valves swap: Bay 2 starts gassing while Bay 1 is emptied and refilled with the yard loader.

Sell the fines

With free lime at 1% or less, the fines no longer swell and can go to fill or road base. The main value is selling fines that cost money to landfill; the stored CO₂ is a small extra. Whether the processor pays to landfill them today is still to confirm.

The gas path
Gas comes off the mill's own stack, is cooled and dried, passes through whichever bay is gassing, and goes back to the stack.
What is mine, and what is next

What is mine

The design around the chemistry.

The carbonation chemistry is Nippon Steel's published work (2008). My part is the design: cooling the gas to a controlled dew point so water does not soak the bed, and two bays that take turns so the gas never has to stop.

Planned experiment

Test F5

Free lime and swelling of carbonated fines in a covered bed, aiming to beat Nippon's 1.88% free lime. It follows the work Dr. McCormick sets. A leach test of the finished fines comes with it. None of it has been run yet.
