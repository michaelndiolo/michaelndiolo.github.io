Project 01 · at the steel mill

The fines lung

Fresh steel-slag fines swell when they get wet, so the fine sizes cannot go into roads or concrete. A covered bay and cooled stack gas can age them in days, not months. Modeled on paper: nothing is built and nothing is measured.

What I changed

Nippon Steel gassed damp fines with CO₂ in 2008, but its static bed on real exhaust stalled when condensation wetted the slag: free lime fell only from 3.2% to 1.9%, short of the 1% target. I chill the gas so it arrives just under the bed's temperature, add water only where probes ask, and end each cycle on CO₂ sensors. The aim is Nippon's result with no mixer.

The catch

It only pays if a processor really pays to age or landfill these fines, and I have not confirmed that. It stores about 4 t of CO₂ a day, so it is a fines process, not a carbon one. Test F5 decides.

What is mine, and what is next

What is mine

The design around the chemistry.

The carbonation chemistry is Nippon Steel's published work (2008). My part is the design: cooling the gas to a controlled dew point so water does not soak the bed, and two bays that take turns so the gas never has to stop.

Planned experiment

Test F5

Free lime and swelling of carbonated fines in a covered bed, aiming to beat Nippon's 1.88% free lime. It follows the work Dr. McCormick sets. A leach test of the finished fines comes with it. None of it has been run yet.
