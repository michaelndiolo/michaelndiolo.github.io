Four diagrams of the OlivAir reactor: the full vessel cutaway, the Zone 0 acid delivery geometry, how Zone 0 sorts grains by residence time, and the hollow baffle.

The reactor, figure by figure

Read alongside the OlivAir overview. The labels say “acid”; in the CO₂ design, the acid is carbonic acid, which is CO₂ dissolved in water.

Figure 1

Reactor cutaway — the four functional zones

One vertical vessel with four functional zones: the Zone 0 confinement wall, the acid distribution floor and sparger manifolds, the low mineral feed chute, the impeller above Zone 0, and the hollow baffles along the wall.

Platform architectureZone 0–3
Figure 2

Zone 0 acid delivery geometry

The confinement wall's nozzle ring and the floor distribution manifold combine into a single net upward superficial velocity (U₀) governing the reactor's hydrodynamic classification behavior.

Hydrodynamic classificationZone 0
Figure 3

Zone 0 hydrodynamic classification

Coarse, unreacted grains sit low in the confinement zone and stay in the acid longer. Finer, partly dissolved grains are carried upward to the impeller and into Zone 1.

Hydrodynamic classification chamberZone 0
Figure 4

Hollow baffle — plan view

A top-down view through one baffle unit at Zone 2 height. Wall 1 is longer than Wall 2, and a slanted, louvered front face joins them. Wall 1's extension deflects flow. The liquid circling the vessel has to meet Wall 2, the shorter wall, first.
