Project 02 · at the mine

The mine drainage reactor

Steel-slag fines used in place of quicklime to neutralize acid mine water, in a two-stage pulsed slag bed. Modeled on paper: nothing is built and nothing is measured.

← OlivAirAlso: the fines lung →Next: the gas rule →
What the reactor does

Fine steel slag neutralizes acid mine water in place of quicklime. The hard part is keeping the grains clean and getting the spent ones out.

Slag grains dissolve as they work, so the bed is a moving mix of fresh grains, shrinking grains and iron oxide that does not react at all. The reactor sorts them with the water itself, and catches the dead grains before they can foul the bed.

Aeration between the two stages and a clarifier at the end are ordinary equipment. What I designed is the part in between.

What I changed

Slag has been tried on mine water, including a fluidized slag bed at Ohio University (2006), and plain slag beds armor over and go inert. The two-stage pulsed bed draws off spent grains so the bed never fully coats, catches the iron floc inside the vessel, and sets contact time so the pH does not overshoot (slag leachate reached pH 11.7 in tests).

The catch

It only wins where the spent slag goes back as fill, or was going to landfill anyway. A small site (1,000 m³ a day) loses about $257 a day, and the reactor must install for under about $1.6M. Test W2 measures how much of the slag actually gets used.

Already known

Neutralizing acid water with slag is known; the margin over lime is what is being tested.
