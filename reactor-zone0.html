Zone 0

The bottom of the vessel and the part that is new. It decides how long each grain stays in the acid.

Zone 0

Zone 0 is a narrower chamber at the floor. A confinement wall sits around the impeller shaft and walls it off from the rest of the vessel. The mineral feed chute enters low and to one side. Acid comes in from below and from the wall, and the grains sit in a bed that the acid holds in rough, constant motion. The impeller above decides when a grain leaves.

Acid delivery

Floor and wall nozzles

Acid reaches Zone 0 by two independent paths. A floor manifold of nozzles points straight up. The confinement wall is hollow and holds five rows of nozzles angled up and inward.

The two flows add into one net upward velocity, U₀ = Q₀ / A₀: the flow divided by the chamber cross-section.

Acid delivery

The jets shear the grain surface

The acid enters as jets, not as a slow trickle. A fast jet shears the liquid at a grain surface, replaces it with fresh acid, and knocks loose the reaction product that builds up there. The force comes from the velocity, which is why a pressure washer cleans a wall.

The plan is to dissolve the CO₂ into the water before it reaches the nozzles, so what hits the grains is acid. How much this speeds dissolution in a bed of moving grains has not been measured, and the cost model gives it no credit. Pre-dissolving the CO₂ also costs about $30 more per tonne in the model, so the jets have to earn that back. See the numbers.

Classification

Coarse grains stay low, fines rise

The net upward velocity opposes settling. Coarse, unreacted grains sit low and stay in the acid longer. A grain that has dissolved is finer and expected to be lighter and more porous, so it is carried upward more easily until the impeller reaches it.

The height of the bed is adjustable, and that height is the control: it sets how long a grain stays in the dissolving region. In the cost model the gate saves about $30 per tonne against a plain stirred tank, where every grain gets a different time ($304 against $335 gross). That saving comes from a description of how grains behave, not from data.

Impeller lift

The impeller picks grains up

The impeller hangs from the roof on a shaft that runs down through Zone 0 with no support beneath it. Its swept diameter matches the confinement wall. It intercepts material rising out of Zone 0 and throws it into Zone 1.

What is not known yet
  • How long real slag needs to dissolve in carbonic acid, and how that depends on grain size and pressure. This is the biggest unknown.
  • Whether reacted grains really do become lighter and rise.
  • How many grains leave early. The model assumes 5%.
  • Whether calcium carbonate stays out of the bed. Fresh acid should keep the bed acidic, but this is untested.
  • How much the jets speed up dissolution.
