Zones 1 to 3 and baffles

Above Zone 0 the pH rises zone by zone, and the hollow baffles are meant to do the catching.

Zones 1 to 3

Fresh acid is added continuously at the bottom, so the pH is lowest there and highest at the top. The zones are functional, not separate tanks, and each has its own residence time.

Zone 1

The impeller feeds Zone 1

The impeller throws lifted grains into Zone 1, which holds four hollow baffles. Here the pH rises from about 3.6 at the base to about 6. The 3.6 is a calculation for CO₂ at 5 bar in pure water, not a measurement.

Zones 2 and 3

The pH rises to the discharge value

In the upper zones the pH reaches the discharge value, and the extra residence time lets the last metals precipitate before the water leaves. The discharge pH is a design choice. Water carries more calcium per litre at a lower pH, but the dissolved CO₂ that holds it there escapes at the outfall. On the fresh-water route the cost model finds the lowest cost near pH 6.75.

Whether the silica from the slag needs a capture step of its own is an open question.

Two walls and a louvered face

A top-down view through one baffle unit at Zone 2 height. Wall 1 is longer than Wall 2, and a slanted, louvered front face joins them. The difference in wall length sets the slant of the face. Wall 1 has an extension that only deflects flow.

Which way it facesThe liquid circling the vessel has to meet Wall 2, the shorter wall, first. Turned the other way around, the unit does not work.

Patent filed
What it is for

Dead zones that hold liquid and catch solids

Because the liquid meets Wall 2 first, it is pushed in through the louvered face. Inside, it recirculates and stays longer, so fines caught there keep dissolving. As the pH rises through Zone 1, trace metals from the slag are expected to precipitate and be caught on the baffles.

One risk is untested: liquid in the dead zones may sit close to saturation and scale the baffles.
